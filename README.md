# Music Mood Classification
Music mood classification plays a critical role in Music Information Retrieval (MIR), enabling users with person alized recommendations of songs in streaming platforms. With the rise of deep learning, various approaches incorporating lyrics and audio have been on the rise. In this study, we replicate and evaluate the work of Pyrovolakis et al., focusing on their lyric-based and audio-based deep learning models for mood classification across four categories: happy, angry, sad, and relaxed. We used two MoodyLyrics datasets for songs-mood classifications and retrieve lyrics for them via the Lyrics.Ovh API and the Spotify 1 Million Tracks Dataset. We also collected audio previews of songs using the Deezer API. For lyric classification, we implemented models using various embedding techniques (BoW, TF-IDF, Word2Vec, GloVe, and BERT) alongside LSTM, feedforward, and Transformer-based architectures. For audio classification, we extracted six features and trained a CNN model. Our results show that BERT-based lyric models performed best, achieving 62.2% accuracy, while audio-based models underper formed significantly, likely due to limited data and computational constraints. Despite challenges, this project provided us insights into the individual contributions of lyrics and audio to mood classification and highlights future directions for improving deep learning emotion-aware music systems
